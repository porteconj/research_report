---
title: The financial performance of Portuguese football clubs

Football clubs are special enterprises, combining a significant social and cultural role with the financial demands of a competitive business environment. This is particularly evident in Portugal, where football has played a prominent role throughout the country's history and has become an important component of national identity. Their social and cultural relevance, together with their substantial economic contribution, makes the financial sustainability of Portuguese football clubs a matter of broader interest.

This paper examines the determinants of financial performance using financial data from 29 Portuguese football clubs between 2018 and 2023. The analysis focuses on the effects of debt, equity, personnel expenses, and club size, while also accounting for differences in league participation, legal form, and the impact of the COVID-19 pandemic.

The findings indicate that debt, personnel expenses, and club size are the main determinants of financial performance. Higher levels of debt are associated with lower profitability, with a stronger negative effect observed among professional and second-division clubs. Personnel expenses also have a negative impact on financial performance when they account for a larger proportion of operating income. Although investment in players and staff is essential for improving sporting performance, it can undermine financial sustainability when it is not supported by corresponding revenue growth. In contrast, clubs with larger asset bases tend to achieve stronger financial performance, reflecting a greater capacity to generate income and attract investment.

The results further indicate that legal form does not, in itself, significantly influence financial performance. Similarly, the effects of the COVID-19 pandemic were not uniform across clubs. A significant deterioration in financial performance during the pandemic period was observed only among professional clubs when compared with non-professional clubs outside the pandemic period.

Overall, this paper concludes that the financial sustainability of Portuguese football clubs depends primarily on prudent debt management, effective control of personnel expenses relative to operating income, and strategic investment in assets. These priorities are essential not only for the long-term financial viability of clubs but also for safeguarding football's broader contribution to Portuguese society and the national economy.
