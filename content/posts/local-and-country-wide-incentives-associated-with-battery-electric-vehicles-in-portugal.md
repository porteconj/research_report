As a member of the European Union, Portugal has been working to address the challenges of achieving carbon neutrality. This paper focuses on the geographic adoption of electric vehicles in Portugal. In Portugal, use of battery electric vehicles is more concentrated in the metropolitan areas, particularly around Lisbon and Porto, than in other regions such as the Alentejo, as illustrated in the figures below.

What may explain these geographical differences? The concentration of battery electric vehicles may depend on a region’s income, the availability of adequate infrastructure, population density, and policy decisions at both the national and local levels.

![](https://ucarecdn.com/8adb1f3e-1231-4b7c-9634-fd0c1eef8058/ "Density of use of BEV in continental Portugal. Each dot represents the normalized value of the logarithm of the number of battery electric vehicles.")

![](https://ucarecdn.com/41f9887f-6a92-414e-91ed-401b4404f9a0/ "Density of use of BEV in Azores and Madeira. Each dot represents the normalized value of the logarithm of the number of battery electric vehicles.")

The paper studies the relation between fiscal incentives and the spatial concentration of battery electric vehicles. The results suggest that national fiscal incentives alone are not sufficient to explain the observed spatial concentration of battery electric vehicles. However, when local fiscal incentives are considered, there emerges a clear relation: local fiscal incentives are a strong determinant of the spatial concentration of battery electric vehicles.

These results suggest that the geographical distribution of electric vehicles cannot be understood by looking at national incentives alone. Local policies may also matter, particularly when they interact with the economic, demographic and infrastructure characteristics of different regions of the country.
