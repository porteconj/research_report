---
title: When doctors and nurses retire, what happens to hospital care?

Portugal's public hospitals are facing a wave of retirements: the National Health Service expects health professionals retirements to rise by around 30% between 2024 and 2030 compared with previous years. This raises a practical question that gets little attention in the policy debate: when an experienced doctor or nurse leaves for good, what happens to the hospital's ability to treat patients?

To find out, the authors linked every hospital admission in Portugal's public hospitals between 2006 and 2018 with social security records showing exactly when doctors and nurses retired, making possible the tracking of what happens in the weeks following a retirement, hospital by hospital and month by month. 

The headline finding is reassuring: retirements do not lead hospitals to treat fewer patients overall. What changes instead is the mix of care provided. After a doctor retires, hospitals admit relatively fewer patients for planned overnight stays and carry out somewhat fewer surgeries. This effect is strongest in smaller hospitals, which have less staff to cover for the loss and are further from larger university hospitals that supply new recruits. In addition, patients' health does not appear to suffer, at least in the short run: death rates and the share of patients who return to hospital shortly after treatment remain stable. 

Structured interviews with doctors and hospital managers help explain why. When someone retires, hospitals quietly protect the most urgent and serious cases, while postponing less pressing care, calling on overtime, and leaning on internal promotions and trainees to fill the gap.

The lesson for policy isn't that retirements are harmless - they clearly create strain - but that hospitals are already absorbing much of the shock by rationing less urgent care rather than compromising on critical patients. Better anticipation of retirements, and faster replacement, could reduce this hidden cost, particularly for smaller hospitals.
