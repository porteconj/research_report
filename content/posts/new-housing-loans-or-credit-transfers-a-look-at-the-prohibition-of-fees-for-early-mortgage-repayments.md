---
title: "New housing loans or credit transfers: a look at the prohibition of fees
  for early mortgage repayments"
date: 2026-06-09T21:37:41.896Z
categories:
  - Financial Markets
---
In January 2022, 91% of mortgages in Portugal were floating rate mortgages. So, as interest rates began to rise in the spring of 2022, there was widespread concern about the impact on Portuguese households. In response, the government introduced measures to support borrowers, including legislation prohibiting credit institutions from charging fees for early mortgage repayments. 

This paper studies the credit transfers that may have followed the policy change. Credit transfers, defined as the refinancing of an existing mortgage with another credit institution, do not represent new lending but can substantially distort aggregate statistics on new housing loans.

The paper proposes a novel methodology to identify credit transfers using loan-level data from the Central Credit Register (CCR). The paper develops an algorithm that matches newly originated loans with mortgages repaid ahead of schedule, using information on borrowers, loan amounts, timing, and collateral. The methodology combines two complementary approaches: an amount-based matching algorithm for an initial identification and a collateral-based matching algorithm linking loans secured by the same property, after harmonizing reporting practices across institutions. The final framework integrates both approaches to maximize coverage and overcome data limitations.

Applying this methodology shows credit transfers became highly relevant in Portugal during the recent interest rate tightening cycle. In 2023, they represented 27% of the total amount of new housing loans, compared with negligible levels in previous years, and remained elevated in early 2024.

The analysis further indicates that most transferred loans were floating-rate contracts originated in a low-interest-rate environment. After refinancing, households largely shifted toward mixed-rate contracts to reduce interest rate risk while keeping maturities broadly unchanged.
