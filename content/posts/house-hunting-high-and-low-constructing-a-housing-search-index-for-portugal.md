The housing market has been at the center of the Portuguese public debate in recent years. House prices have risen rapidly, raising questions about the causes of price growth. Demand is difficult to observe directly and traditional indicators often capture housing market conditions only after households have already decided to buy.

![](https://ucarecdn.com/abdf63ce-e061-4de0-a7ae-cff21a827518/ "Portuguese house price growth (left scale) and the Housing Search Index (right scale).")

This paper asks whether online search activity can be used to construct a timely index of housing demand. Looking for a home increasingly starts online, and searches made long before a purchase can therefore provide an early signal of households’ interest in buying property. The paper uses Google search data related to the house-buying process to construct a monthly Housing Search Index for Portugal, available since 2004.

T﻿he authors find that the index contains substantial information about future house price growth. When housing-related searches increase, house prices tend to grow more strongly in the following months. Importantly, the index significantly improves predictions of house price growth compared with commonly used indicators such as interest rates, employment and measures of housing supply. Its predictive power is particularly strong over short and medium-term horizons.

Online searches also allow the authors to look beyond aggregate domestic demand. This matters in Portugal, where the share of homes bought by foreign residents increased significantly over the past years. We construct separate indicators of housing interest from abroad and find that foreign demand provides additional information for explaining changes in Portuguese house prices.

Overall, our results suggest that what people search for online can provide a timely window into housing demand. The index is free to build and can be updated almost in real time, while official statistics arrive with a lag. It can therefore complement traditional indicators when assessing developments in the Portuguese housing market.
