This paper asks ow do micro firms combine different ways of innovating, and what difference do these combinations make to the results they achieve? While previous research has often focused on larger organisations, less is known about how micro firms, which make up the majority of businesses in Europe, innovate in practice.

Using new data from Portuguese micro firms, the study shows that innovation is far more common and varied among micro firms than is often assumed. Rather than following a single approach to innovation, firms tend to combine different activities, drawing on both formal and informal sources of knowledge. Some rely mainly on learning through day-to-day experience, problem-solving, and interactions with customers and suppliers. Others complement these activities with more formal sources of knowledge, such as research-related activities, or technical expertise. Several recurring combinations of practices emerge, revealing a much richer and more diverse innovation landscape than is typically recognised.

The study also finds that these combinations matter. Different ways of bringing together innovation activities are associated with different types of outcomes, suggesting that there is no single path to successful innovation. For example, firms that combine practical experience with internal technical and knowledge-building activities are more likely to introduce new products. Firms that combine a broader range of internal and external knowledge sources are more likely to achieve process and organisational innovations. These findings offer a richer understanding of how innovation takes place in micro firms and challenge simplified views of innovation behaviour. They also provide useful insights for policymakers seeking to support innovation in a group of firms that is economically significant but often overlooked.
