---
title: "Effects of decentralisation on educational outcomes: Insights  from the
  2009 and 2015 Portuguese reforms"
date: 2026-09-17T13:12:17.662Z

---
Should decisions about how schools are run sit with the Ministry of Education, or with the municipal council? Supporters argue that giving local governments more say brings services closer to citizens; critics worry it can undermine national equality.

To answer this question, the article studies the effects of two reforms that gradually handed Portuguese municipalities more say over their local schools, first in 2009 and then again in 2015. Portugal offers a rare test, since between 2000 and 2019 around four in ten mainland municipalities voluntarily signed contracts with the Ministry of Education: in 2009 taking on mostly operational tasks such as school maintenance and non-teaching staff, and in 2015 a smaller group of fifteen municipalities taking on broader responsibilities covering school administration and educational planning.

The study tracks all 278 mainland municipalities to see whether these reforms changed pupils' access to public schools, repetition and transition rates, national exam results, and the money municipalities received to cover the added costs. The 2009 reform appears to have lowered repetition rates and increased public-school enrolment in the later years of basic education. 

The 2015 reform, in contrast, shows no measurable effect (though only a small number of municipalities took part, making smaller effects hard to detect). Financial transfers to participating municipalities increased under both reforms, more clearly so after 2009. One likely explanation: the tasks handed to municipalities in 2009 were largely operational (school buildings, transport, and extracurricular activities) rather than about teaching itself. And even under the more ambitious 2015 reform, decisions that arguably matter most for learning, such as who teaches and what gets taught, stayed firmly with the Ministry. As Portugal extended these same responsibilities nationwide in 2018, understanding what changed under these earlier, voluntary reforms is particularly relevant.
